Overview

CII-Bench asks a different question of multimodal models than most image benchmarks do: not what a picture shows, but what it means. It targets deep implication understanding in Chinese images — the implied semantics, emotional undertones, and cultural signals that a literal caption would miss. Performance is reported as Overall accuracy on a 0-100 scale, and that single number anchors the leaderboard on this page.

What separates it from generic image suites is where the material comes from. Images are drawn from the Chinese internet and from Chinese traditional culture, then paired with manually reviewed questions and answers so that the original context survives intact. The design forces models to work with visual culture as it actually circulates — metaphors included — where getting the answer right takes background knowledge, not just perception.

The paper's own findings frame how to read the scores. Current MLLMs still lag far behind humans, and the gap is widest on traditional-culture images and on tasks that demand deeper semantic interpretation. When prompts include emotion hints, performance improves noticeably — evidence that models are not extracting sentiment and cultural cues from the image itself so much as leaning on whatever scaffolding the prompt supplies.

Why it matters

Most vision-language evaluation rewards recognition: identify the object, describe the scene, read the text. Implication is a different skill, and a culturally specific one. Any model serving Chinese-speaking users will constantly meet images whose point is the metaphor rather than the pixels, and CII-Bench's persistent human gap shows that this competence cannot be inferred from strong results on generic perception suites.

Method & evaluation

  • CII-Bench evaluates higher-order understanding of Chinese visual content, focusing on whether models can capture implied semantics, emotions, and cultural signals in Chinese images.
  • Unlike generic image benchmarks, it draws images from the Chinese internet and Chinese traditional culture, and pairs them with manually reviewed questions and answers to preserve authentic Chinese context.
  • The paper shows that current MLLMs still lag far behind humans, especially on traditional-culture images and tasks requiring deeper semantic interpretation.
  • It also finds that emotion hints can noticeably improve performance, indicating that intrinsic understanding of image sentiment and Chinese cultural cues remains weak.

Reading the results

Qwen2-VL-72B leads at 64.4, holding a 3.5-point margin over GLM-4V at 60.9, with Gemini 1.5 Pro another 0.8 back in third. From there the table steps down through InternVL2-40B and Qwen-VL-MAX to GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet, tied at 54.1. The whole field fits inside a 10.3-point band, and the ordering carries a signal: the two systems at the top come from Chinese labs, on material drawn from their home context.

The ceiling matters more than the ordering. Overall runs to 100, and the leader does not reach two-thirds of it, leaving 35.6 points of headroom on exactly the material the paper flags as weakest — traditional culture and deeper semantic reading. When the spread from first to last, 10.3 points, is less than a third of the distance still to go, the leaderboard is describing a shared deficit rather than a race.
