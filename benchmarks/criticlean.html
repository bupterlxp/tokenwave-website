Overview

CriticLean puts a language model in the reviewer's chair. Instead of asking it to produce a formalization, the benchmark presents mathematical reasoning and proof fragments and asks one question: is this correct? Critic ability — the capacity to distinguish sound formal work from flawed formal work — is the entire object of measurement, scored as accuracy (ACC) on a 0 to 100 scale.

The evaluation suite, CriticLeanBench, does not rest on model judgment alone. It combines critic signals with Lean verification and testing, which pulls the setup closer to how formalization actually proceeds: automated checking in the loop, tests alongside it, and a judgment call about whether the work holds up. Critique here is treated as a component of a working pipeline rather than an isolated classification exercise, and that framing shapes what a score on this benchmark means.

The study's headline result is a caution rather than a victory lap. Strong reasoning models take the top of the table, which suggests critic accuracy draws on the same capabilities as extended reasoning itself. But overall accuracy remains, in the paper's own assessment, far from the level that would let these systems replace human review — a critic that is usually right is not yet a critic you can leave alone.

Why it matters

Formal mathematics already has a mechanical judge — the Lean checker. CriticLeanBench's design quietly argues that this is not enough: it keeps critic signals, verification, and testing as separate components of the workflow, treating machine checking as necessary but not sufficient. What the critic supplies is the judgment layer, and whether a model can supply it determines whether formalization pipelines can filter their own output at scale.

Method & evaluation

  • This work studies critic ability in mathematical formalization, evaluating whether models can distinguish correct from incorrect reasoning or proof fragments.
  • CriticLeanBench combines critic signals, Lean verification, and testing, making the setup closer to realistic formalization workflows.
  • The results show that strong reasoning models perform best, but overall accuracy is still far from replacing human review.

Reading the results

Gemini 2.5 Pro leads at 89.2 ACC, with QwQ-32B second at 86.4. The gap between them — 2.8 points — is exactly as large as the gap between second and sixth place, where four Alibaba models and DeepSeek-R1 sit between 86.4 and 83.6. The whole field spans just 5.6 points, a tight cluster by the standards of a 0 to 100 accuracy scale.

Read the cluster two ways. Every model tested clears 83, so mid-80s critic accuracy is broadly attainable for strong reasoning systems; nothing here separates the pack dramatically. But the leader still sits 10.8 points below the ceiling, and in a formalization pipeline each of those missing points is a wrong verdict waved through or a sound proof rejected. The paper's conclusion that human review is not yet replaceable follows directly from that arithmetic.
