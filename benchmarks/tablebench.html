Overview

TableBench asks what happens when table question answering stops being lookup. Drawn from realistic industrial scenarios, its questions demand fact checking, numerical reasoning, data analysis, and visualization — four capability groups that fan out into 18 subfields. The benchmark was assembled through scenario analysis, manual annotation, and explicit task-complexity design, so difficulty here is engineered to match real-world demands rather than inherited from whatever data happened to be available.

Scoring on this page follows a single protocol. The underlying paper compares textual, symbolic, and program-based reasoning strategies, introducing TableInstruct and TABLELLM to study them; we fix Overall under the TCoT setting, taken from the paper's main results table, as the primary leaderboard. That choice keeps the core structured-data reasoning challenge intact while avoiding the complexity of mixing protocols that measure different things.

The headline finding is sobering. Across both open-source and proprietary systems, the experiments show current models falling short of practical table-analysis needs, with even the strongest noticeably behind humans. Read this way, TableBench is less a race than a gap measurement — a running estimate of how far structured-data reasoning still has to travel before it is dependable in industrial settings.

Why it matters

Tables are where organizations keep the numbers that decisions actually rest on, and most of the useful questions about them are not lookups. A model that can check a claim against a table, run the arithmetic, and carry an analysis through to a visualization is doing the work analysts do daily. TableBench's insistence on task complexity drawn from real scenarios makes its scores a better proxy for that work than cell-retrieval benchmarks can be.

Reading the results

GPT-4 Turbo leads at 51.32, with GPT-4o less than a point behind at 50.39 — effectively a tie at the top, and neither clears much more than half of the 0-100 range. DeepSeek's two entries hold the next tier at 47.22 and 44.26, with Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct close behind at 43.85. The full field spans 13.2 points down to Qwen2-72B-Instruct at 38.13.

The shape below the top matters as much as the leader. The top five sit within 7.5 points of each other before a 5.3-point drop to Yi-Large and Qwen2-72B-Instruct, which are separated by less than half a point. And with the best score just past the midpoint of a 0-100 scale — and the paper placing even the strongest systems noticeably behind humans — nearly half the range is unclaimed.
