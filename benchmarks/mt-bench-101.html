Overview

Chat leaderboards tend to compress an entire conversation into one number, which hides exactly where a dialogue goes wrong. MT-Bench-101 was built against that habit. Drawing on analysis of real multi-turn conversations, it organizes multi-turn instruction following into a three-level ability taxonomy spanning 13 tasks, then evaluates models across 1,388 dialogues and 4,208 individual turns.

The taxonomy is the point. Because each dialogue is tagged to a specific task and abilities are tracked across 4,208 turns, the benchmark shows not just whether a model converses well overall but how its performance holds up as a conversation progresses, and on which tasks it slips. Scores keep the paper's original 10-point scale, and TokenWave's leaderboard uses the paper's Avg directly — the benchmark is already a fine-grained decomposition, so there is nothing to recombine.

The paper's most pointed finding is that common alignment techniques and chat-oriented designs do not clearly improve multi-turn ability. Whatever those interventions optimize, it is not the capability this benchmark isolates. That result reframes real conversational interaction: not a byproduct of chat tuning that comes along for free, but a distinct and still-open challenge that has to be measured on its own terms.

Why it matters

A model that follows an instruction cleanly in isolation can still lose the thread three turns into a real exchange, and a single aggregate chat score cannot tell you which failure you are looking at. MT-Bench-101's design — a taxonomy of 13 tasks, with ability tracked across turns — turns that vague unease into something diagnosable. For anyone deploying a conversational system, that is the difference between knowing a model's score and knowing its failure modes.

Method & evaluation

  • MT-Bench-101 is designed for fine-grained evaluation of multi-turn dialogue, addressing the fact that prior dialogue benchmarks are often too coarse or too focused on single-turn settings.
  • By analyzing real multi-turn conversations, the paper builds a three-level ability taxonomy spanning 13 tasks, 1,388 dialogues, and 4,208 turns.
  • This allows the authors to compare models not only on overall dialogue quality but also on how their abilities change across turns within different tasks.
  • The study finds that common alignment techniques and chat-oriented designs do not clearly improve multi-turn ability, leaving real conversational interaction as a major challenge for LLMs.

Reading the results

GPT-4 leads at 8.86 on the 10-point scale, 0.76 ahead of Yi-34B at 8.10 — the widest gap anywhere in the table. Below it the field compresses: Yi-34B, GPT-3.5 Turbo at 7.99, and Qwen-14B at 7.82 sit within 0.28 of one another, with Mixtral-8x7B trailing at 7.38. The full five-model spread is 1.48 points, and GPT-4's margin alone accounts for just over half of it.

Even the leader leaves 1.14 points on the table against a perfect 10, and the paper's own analysis suggests that margin will not close as a side effect of better chat tuning, since alignment techniques did not clearly help multi-turn ability. With five models scored, the ranking is less a settled order than an early map: one clear leader, a crowded middle, and a ceiling nobody is near.
