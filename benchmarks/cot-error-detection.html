Overview

Long chain-of-thought models produce reasoning traces that run for pages, and somewhere in those pages the logic can quietly go wrong. DeltaBench asks a pointed question: given a long CoT generated by an o1-like model, can another model locate the errors inside it? The traces span math, coding, and general reasoning, and each one is annotated at the process level, so evaluation targets individual steps rather than the final answer.

That shift — from final-answer correctness to step-level critique — changes what the benchmark can see. A model may reach the right conclusion through a flawed derivation, or fail despite mostly sound work; grading only the endpoint hides both. DeltaBench's process annotations let the paper study the effectiveness and efficiency of long CoTs directly, and compare process reward models against critic models on the same fine-grained error-detection task.

Scoring on this page uses F1 as the primary metric, on a 0-100 scale where higher is better. The paper's first-error and any-error detection accuracies are kept as auxiliary measures; F1 leads the board because it balances finding genuine mistakes against flagging sound steps, which is the whole job of a critic.

Why it matters

As reasoning traces get longer, verifying them by hand stops scaling, and the field's answer — process reward models and automated critics — is only useful if those verifiers actually catch mistakes. DeltaBench measures exactly that link in the chain. Its central finding cuts against a comfortable assumption: long reasoning traces are not inherently reliable, and current process supervision still struggles in realistic long-CoT settings. Any pipeline that trusts a verifier should want this number first.

Reading the results

GPT-4 Turbo 128K leads at 69.17 F1, roughly 18 points clear of GPT-4o at 50.89. Below that the board compresses: DeepSeek-V3 (42.39), Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct (42.23), and DeepSeek-R1 (40.78) sit within 1.7 points of one another. That last name is the detail worth pausing on — DeepSeek-R1, itself a long-reasoning model, audits long reasoning no better than the general-purpose instruct models beside it.

The full spread runs 57.5 points, from the leader down to GPT-3.5 Turbo at 11.65, and even the leader stops more than 30 points short of the metric's ceiling. Scale shows within families too — Qwen2.5 gains about 11.5 points moving from 7B to 32B — but nothing on the board suggests error detection is close to solved.
