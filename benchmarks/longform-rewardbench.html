Testing whether reward models can still separate good from bad when the responses run long.

Overview

Most reward-model benchmarks judge preferences over short responses. Long-form RewardBench moves the contest to the regime where modern models actually spend their tokens: extended outputs across QA, RAG, chat, writing, and reasoning. Each subset probes whether a reward model can still rank a long response correctly, and performance is folded into a single Long-form-RM Score on a 0–100 scale, higher being better.

The shift matters because the evidence a judge relies on changes with length. In a short answer, quality is legible almost at a glance; in a long one, a scorer has to hold structure, consistency, and grounding across the whole span. The authors report that the resulting landscape differs from traditional RewardBench — strength on short preference pairs does not simply carry over — and that robustness in long contexts remains an open problem.

Why it matters

Reward models are the steering mechanism for post-training: they decide which sampled response gets reinforced and which best-of-n candidate ships. As deployed systems drift toward long outputs — grounded RAG answers, extended writing, multi-step reasoning — a reward model that only ranks reliably at short lengths quietly corrupts that signal exactly where it is hardest to audit. Benchmarking the long-form regime directly is the prerequisite for trusting it.

Method & evaluation

  • This work focuses on reward models for long-form outputs rather than ordinary short-response preference judgments.
  • It covers QA, RAG, chat, writing, and reasoning subsets to test ranking quality on long responses.
  • The results show that the long-form reward-model landscape differs from traditional RewardBench, and robustness in long contexts remains challenging.

Reading the results

The board is a photo finish. Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.2-3B leads at 74.9, with GPT-4.1 at 74.6 and Claude Opus 4 and AllenAI's Llama-3.1-70B RM tied at 74.4; Gemini 2.5 Flash closes the table at 73.6. All six systems sit inside a 1.3-point band, and the leader's margin over second place is just 0.3.

Two things stand out in that cluster. A dedicated 3B reward model finishing ahead of frontier generalist judges suggests that scale is not what long-form ranking currently rewards. And with the top score at 74.9 on a 0–100 metric, roughly a quarter of the range is still unclaimed — the compressed spread reads less like a solved problem than like six systems hitting the same wall.
