Overview

ACADREASON swaps exam questions for the real thing: research problems drawn from genuine academic work in computer science, economics, law, mathematics, and philosophy. Where a typical benchmark rewards retrieving a known answer, these tasks are open-ended by construction — the model has to pull material from multiple sources and sustain a line of reasoning over a long horizon before anything resembling an answer takes shape.

Scoring uses the average score from the paper's main results table, on a 0 to 100 scale where higher is better. The paper reports each result as a solved count alongside an average score; TokenWave tracks the average-score term, which captures graded progress on problems too hard for a clean solve. The headline finding is blunt: even leading models achieve limited overall performance on genuine academic reasoning.

The benchmark sits in the search-agent category for a reason. Multi-source synthesis is not incidental to these problems; it is the work. A research question in law or economics rarely resolves from a single passage, so the evaluation implicitly measures how well a model can locate, weigh, and combine evidence — the loop that separates a research assistant from an answer engine.

Why it matters

The benchmark is built on an explicit contrast. Exam-style evaluation rewards producing a known answer in a fixed format; research demands open-ended exploration, synthesis across sources, and reasoning that stays coherent over a long horizon. Those are the capabilities that matter if models are to assist actual scholarship in fields as different as mathematics and law — and they are the ones ACADREASON isolates by using real research problems as the testbed.

Method & evaluation

  • This work uses real academic research problems as a challenging testbed across computer science, economics, law, mathematics, and philosophy.
  • Unlike exam-style benchmarks, ACADREASON emphasizes open-ended research tasks, multi-source synthesis, and long-horizon reasoning.
  • The results show that even leading models still achieve limited overall performance on genuine academic reasoning problems.

Reading the results

GPT-5 leads at 40.5, a full 7.1 points ahead of o3 at 33.4 — and OpenAI holds the entire top three, with GPT-OSS close behind at 32.2. Below that the board compresses: DeepSeek-V3.1, Claude 4 Sonnet, and DeepSeek-R1 sit within a single point of each other, 24.8 down to 23.8. The whole field spans just 16.7 points, all of it in the bottom half of the scale.

Still, the ordering matters less than the altitude. Even the leader leaves nearly 60 points of the 0-100 range unclaimed, which is the paper's own conclusion in numeric form: leading models still achieve limited performance on genuine academic reasoning. The interesting race here is not between labs but between the field and the problems.
