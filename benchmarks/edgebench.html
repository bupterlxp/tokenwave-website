Abstract

EdgeBench measures how agents learn from real environments over ultra-long horizons: 134 real-world tasks across six domains, each supporting 12 or more hours of continuous operation. Performance follows a log-sigmoid law in interaction time (R² = 0.998) — the benchmark behind the environment-learning scaling law.

Contributions

  • 134 expert-built real-world tasks spanning science and ML, systems engineering, optimization, professional knowledge work, formal math, and games; experts averaged 57.2 hours per task.
  • Every task supports 12+ hours of agent operation, with extended runs beyond 72 hours; 51 tasks are public and 83 held out against contamination.
  • The SForge harness isolates the agent's work container from ephemeral judge containers, giving granular feedback in a closed improvement loop.

Method & evaluation

  • Agents work in an executable environment, submit repeatedly, and receive pass rates and failing-test feedback until timeout.
  • The best submission across the run is the task score.
  • Across ~38,000 hours of interaction, score follows a log-sigmoid function of environment time; learning speed roughly doubles every three months across model generations.

Evaluation metrics

Score @12h — Higher is better. Best-submission score after 12 hours of environment interaction, averaged over tasks. Score range: [0, 100].
