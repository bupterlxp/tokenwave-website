Overview

CodeSimpleQA tests a part of coding intelligence that execution benchmarks often blur: whether a model knows concrete facts about programming languages, frameworks, systems, algorithms, databases, and software practice. Its 1,498 human-curated question-answer pairs are split between 782 English and 716 Chinese items, cover more than 15 programming languages and 21 computer-science domains, and are checked against authoritative technical sources.

The paper reports separate domain-level F1 tables for Chinese and English. To keep the site comparison bilingual, TokenWave averages each model's English Avg. and Chinese Avg. values. The resulting Bilingual mean F1 is therefore a transparent derived score, not a third metric claimed by the authors; the original language-specific values remain traceable to Tables 3 and 4.

Why it matters

A model can produce executable code while still hallucinating an API contract, a language rule, or a systems fact. Those errors are especially costly in code review, debugging, migration, and technical support, where an authoritative-sounding false detail can steer an entire implementation. CodeSimpleQA isolates that factual layer and, by testing both English and Chinese, shows whether technical knowledge transfers across the two languages rather than hiding behind an English-only aggregate.

Contributions

  • The benchmark contains 1,498 human-curated question-answer pairs grounded in programming and computer-science documentation.
  • Its bilingual coverage spans English and Chinese, more than 15 programming languages, and 21 computer-science domains.
  • The paper separates factual code knowledge from code-generation execution and also releases a 66.9M-example instruction corpus for training.

Method & evaluation

  • CodeSimpleQA contains 782 English and 716 Chinese question-answer pairs whose answers are verified against authoritative technical sources.
  • Questions cover programming languages, frameworks, systems, algorithms, databases, software engineering, security, and other computer-science topics.
  • The paper reports F1 by domain and an overall Avg. for each language in Tables 3 and 4.
  • For one bilingual leaderboard, TokenWave takes the arithmetic mean of each model's English Avg. and Chinese Avg.; this derived score is not a separate metric reported by the paper.

Evaluation metrics

Bilingual mean F1 — Higher is better. TokenWave-derived arithmetic mean of the English Avg. and Chinese Avg. F1 columns reported in arXiv Tables 3 and 4. Score range: [0, 100].

Reading the results

GPT-5 leads the bilingual aggregation at 65.05, followed by o3 at 61.65 — a 3.40-point gap. GPT-5 Mini is another 6.00 points back at 55.65, while o4-mini and the two Claude Sonnet 4 variants occupy the next band from 53.30 to 51.90. The top-ten spread reaches 16.35 points, yet even the leader leaves almost 35 points of the scale unclaimed. On this benchmark, factual programming knowledge remains a distinct and visibly unsolved capability.
