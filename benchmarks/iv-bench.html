Overview

IV-Bench asks a question most video benchmarks never pose: what happens when the video alone cannot answer? Each task pairs a video with a supporting image, and the benchmark is built around image necessity — the still image is not decoration but evidence, required to reach the correct answer. The result is an evaluation of image-grounded video perception and reasoning, a setting the work argues reflects how some video-understanding tasks actually arise.

The tasks span two axes, perception and reasoning, so a model must both extract what the image and video jointly show and draw conclusions that neither source supports on its own. Scoring rolls up into a single Overall score on a 0-100 scale, and the leaderboard on this page tracks the Overall column of the paper's main results table.

IV-Bench sits within long video understanding, where models already contend with extended temporal context; requiring an external image on top of that tightens the problem further. The paper's own results show substantial headroom even for strong models — a signal that image-grounded video reasoning is not a free byproduct of general multimodal training but a capability still to be built.

Why it matters

Most video benchmarks treat the video as self-contained: everything needed to answer sits somewhere in the frames. IV-Bench's image-necessity constraint tests a different skill — binding an external, static reference to a temporal stream. The work argues this is closer to how video tasks actually arise, and the design has teeth: a model that ignores the image has no path to the correct answer.

For anyone deploying multimodal systems, the numbers here are a caution. The strongest model on this board scores under 29 of 100, so a pipeline that assumes video and reference images will be integrated reliably is assuming a capability the field does not yet have.

Method & evaluation

  • This work argues that some video-understanding tasks require supporting images in addition to the video stream itself.
  • IV-Bench covers both perception and reasoning, and uses image necessity to make the tasks more realistic and challenging.
  • The results show substantial headroom even for strong models, indicating that image-grounded video reasoning remains difficult.

Reading the results

Qwen2.5-VL-72B leads at 28.9, but “leads” overstates the separation: InternVL2.5-78B sits 0.3 behind at 28.6, and the top five — including Gemini 2 Pro at 27.7 and Gemini 2 Flash at 27.4 — are packed within 2.3 points. Only Qwen2.5-VL-32B, at 23.0, falls out of the cluster, giving the board a total spread of 5.9. The largest models from Alibaba and OpenGVLab currently edge out Google's entries, though at margins this thin the ordering is fragile.

The more telling number is the ceiling. With Overall running 0 to 100, the leader captures less than 29 points of the available range, leaving more than 71 unclaimed. Scale helps within a family — the 72B Qwen beats its 32B sibling by 5.9 points, the 78B InternVL beats the 38B by 2.0 — but nothing on the board suggests scale alone will close that distance.
