Overview

Most software-engineering benchmarks hand the agent a working environment and ask for a patch. Multi-Docker-Eval removes that gift. It evaluates whether a model can take a diverse repository and bring it to a test-ready state on its own, across different languages and setups — the work that stands between a fresh checkout and a passing test run. This page reports F2P, the first-to-pass rate, under the SWE-Builder framework.

The framing extends software-engineering evaluation past the patch and into construction itself. The metric, F2P, is a first-to-pass rate: it credits a model only when the environment it builds actually reaches a passing test state. Scores run on a 0-100 scale, and the study's central finding is blunt — environment building remains a major bottleneck for agentic software engineering, with large differences across models in both success rate and cost.

Why it matters

Every agentic coding result silently depends on someone having built the harness first. On real repositories nobody has done that work: an agent that cannot stand up its own environment cannot verify its own patches, no matter how good its edits are. Multi-Docker-Eval isolates that prerequisite and prices it, which is why its scores read less like a coding ranking and more like a ceiling on end-to-end autonomy.

Reading the results

DeepSeek-V3.1 leads at 37.72, but only by a tenth of a point over Kimi-K2-0905 at 37.62 — effectively a tie at the top. The five leading systems, from four different labs, sit within 3.6 points of one another; the field then drops 7.1 points to GPT-OSS-120B at 27. Read the cluster rather than the ranks: capability here has converged across labs, and nobody clears 38 on a 0-100 scale. Most environments still defeat even the best model tested.
