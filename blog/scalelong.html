Minutes are easy; hours are not. ScaleLong stress-tests long-video understanding with questions whose evidence is scattered across durations that defeat frame-sampling shortcuts.

Most "long video" benchmarks are long the way a hallway is long — you still see the far end from where you stand. ScaleLong is long the way a workday is long: the thing you need at hour one only makes sense because of something that happened in minute three, and nothing in between announces which moments matter.

Four timescales, one clip

The benchmark decomposes understanding into clip, shot, event, and story levels and asks questions at each scale over the same footage. That structure exposes a failure mode single-scale suites hide: models that are sharp at the shot level often collapse at the story level, because sparse frame sampling preserves texture while destroying narrative.

  • Clip-level: perception — what is on screen right now.
  • Shot-level: local continuity — what just changed.
  • Event-level: causal grouping — what is happening.
  • Story-level: global integration — why it all happened.
