T2AV-Compass: Measuring Synchronized Audio-Video Generation

2026-04-20 · T2AV-Compass · 1 min read

Generating video and audio together is harder than generating either alone. T2AV-Compass evaluates semantic alignment, temporal synchrony, and perceptual quality for text-to-audio-video systems.

A door slams and the sound arrives a beat late. Nothing else is wrong with the clip — and yet everything is wrong with the clip. Human perception forgives soft textures and odd lighting long before it forgives broken synchrony, which is why text-to-audio-video generation cannot be graded as two separate tracks.

Five axes, one verdict

T2AV-Compass evaluates generation systems jointly on video quality, audio quality, cross-modal alignment, instruction following, and realism. The headline number is the average of a structured subjective evaluation — closer to how audiences actually judge generated media than any single automated proxy.

A crowded summit is a finding

Barely two points separate the top four systems. When a leaderboard compresses like this, it usually means the shared failure modes — here, fine-grained lip/action synchrony and faithful instruction following under composition — are the real frontier, and every lab is stuck on the same ones.

  • Alignment errors dominate perceived failures even when per-modality quality is high.
  • Instruction following degrades fastest on prompts that couple sound events to visual events.
  • Realism scores separate systems less than synchrony scores do — the uncanny valley has moved into the audio track.

For anyone building with generative video, the takeaway is blunt: pick systems by their synchrony behavior, because that is where the ranking is actually decided.
